Ditto — Privacy Policy

Effective November 1, 2026

The short version

Ditto collects no data: we never receive anything about you or your squares. On Android, Google Play's billing library, which handles the subscription, sends Google basic diagnostic reports about the billing service (details below). If you choose to link a web browser to your phone, your board travels between them end-to-end encrypted, so we can't read it either.

What Ditto stores, and where

The text snippets you save in Ditto are stored only on your own device — in the app's private storage on iPhone and Android, or in your browser's local storage on the web version. They are never uploaded, synced, or transmitted to us. They leave your device only when you send them yourself, in encrypted form that we cannot read if you turn on Link to the web, or, on Android, in Android's own device backup if you have it turned on (see Ditto on Android).

Ditto 1.5 and later on iPhone, and Ditto 1.1 and later on Android, also keep a count of the squares you tap, how many words they put in and the day counting started, for My stats. The count stays on your device (on Android, Android's own device backup also includes it if you have that turned on). It isn't part of Link to the web, Share board or an exported backup file, and we never see it.

What we collect

Nothing. Ditto has no accounts, no sign-up, no analytics, no advertising, and no tracking of any kind. Apart from Link to the web, which you have to turn on yourself, the iPhone app, the Android app and their keyboards make no network requests of their own. We never receive any information about you. The one exception on Android is Google's own: Google Play's billing library sends Google basic diagnostic reports about the billing service, described under Ditto on Android. The iPhone app has nothing like it.

Link to the web (Ditto 1.4 and later on iPhone, Ditto 1.1 and later on Android, only if you turn it on)

Link to the web lets you edit your board in a browser at dittotap.com/link. It stays off until you open Settings › Link to the web in Ditto and scan the code shown on that page (or paste its link). The code holds a secret key. It goes from your computer screen to your phone and is never sent to us.

Your phone encrypts your board and its pictures with that key before they leave the phone, and your browser decrypts them on your computer. In between they wait on our relay as scrambled data that we cannot read. The relay runs on Cloudflare, which sees ordinary connection details such as IP addresses to deliver the traffic; we don't use them to identify you. The relay erases everything 30 days after the last change, or straight away when you tap Unlink on the phone or on the web page. The web page keeps the key in your browser so it stays linked, and Unlink removes it.

Your phone talks to the relay only while Ditto is open. On iPhone, Ditto checks for changes when you open it and sends yours about two seconds after an edit. On Android, Ditto syncs when it opens, about two seconds after an edit, and once more right away if you leave Ditto with an unsent change. Neither syncs in the background otherwise.

If you also turn on Use my phone's squares (in Settings on dittotap.com, or on the dittotap.com/link page), the web app at dittotap.com shows that same board in that browser, through the same encrypted link, and edits you make there go back to your phone the same way. Turned off, the web app uses only the squares stored in your browser.

Link to the web never sends your chat buttons, your settings or anything you type with the keyboard. The Ditto keyboard never sends what you type anywhere, with or without Link to the web; on iPhone it has no internet access at all.

The Ditto keyboard

The Ditto keyboard shows your squares inside other apps and types a square's text when you tap it. It works with iOS "Full Access" switched off (the default — you can see the switch in Settings › Ditto › Keyboards) and never uses the internet, so it has no way to send anything anywhere. It reads only your own squares, and your My stats count, from Ditto's private storage. iOS shows the same general notice about third-party keyboards for every keyboard that is not Apple's; in Ditto's case nothing you type is stored or transmitted — the keyboard looks at the text field only to decide whether to add a blank line before the next snippet. The only thing the keyboard keeps is a count of the squares you tap with it and how many words they put in, so My stats can show the time Ditto saved you; that count never leaves your iPhone.

Ditto on Android

The Android app works the same way: your squares live in the app's private storage on your device. Link to the web is the only part of Ditto's own code that goes online, and only after you link a computer; the board and its pictures are encrypted on the phone first, as described above. Apart from that, the only networking component inside the app is Google Play's billing library, which handles the Ditto Pro subscription.

With Link to the web on, Ditto syncs when it opens, about two seconds after an edit, and once more right away if you leave Ditto with an unsent change. It never syncs in the background otherwise. The link key is locked by a key in the Android Keystore, stays on that device and is left out of Android's backups and phone-to-phone transfers, so a new or restored phone starts unlinked. Ditto asks for camera access only when you tap Scan the code, uses the camera only to read the code on the screen, and saves or sends no picture. The relay erases everything 30 days after the last change, or straight away when you tap Unlink, exactly as on iPhone.

That billing library also sends Google basic diagnostic reports about the billing service (for example device model, Android version and whether a purchase step succeeded). Ditto never receives this information; it is handled by Google under the Google Play Terms of Service and Google's Privacy Policy. Nothing you type, and none of your squares, is included.

The Ditto keyboard on Android reads only your own squares and types a square's text when you tap it. It never sends what you type anywhere, with or without Link to the web; the only thing it may ask, through the Google Play app on your phone, is whether your subscription is active. Android shows a standard notice ("this input method may be able to collect all the text you type") for every keyboard that is not Google's own; in Ditto's case nothing you type is stored, analyzed, or transmitted — the keyboard looks at the text field only to choose a blank line or a space before the next square and to keep Select on the squares it just typed; it keeps none of it. It also counts the squares you tap, for My stats; that count stays on the device. If you have Android's own backup turned on, your board, your settings and chat buttons, your My stats count and the dates of your free months and subscription are included in the device backup kept in your Google account, like any other app's data; pictures on squares are not. Moving to a new phone with Android's cable or Wi-Fi transfer (Android 12 and later) also brings the pictures. The Link to the web key never leaves the device, so a new or restored phone starts unlinked. Settings › Backup › Export board sends a backup file through the app you choose, and Save to this device… saves it where you choose; Ditto never uploads it itself.

Ditto Pro subscription

New downloads get Ditto free for their first 4 months (anyone who started using Ditto on iPhone before version 1.4 keeps a full free year); after that, Ditto Pro is a yearly subscription bought through Apple's App Store (iPhone) or Google Play (Android). Apple or Google handles the payment. We never receive your name, payment details, Apple ID or Google account — only an anonymous receipt that says a subscription is active, which is kept on your device. You can manage or cancel the subscription in Settings › Apple Account › Subscriptions on iPhone, or in Google Play › Subscriptions on Android.

Sharing

If you use "Share my list", the snippets you chose to share are encoded into the link itself and travel only through whatever channel you send that link with (for example, your own messages app). We never see them. On iPhone and Android, Share board puts the squares you leave checked inside the link itself, after the # that browsers never send to a server, so nothing is uploaded when someone opens it; or it makes a file that travels only through the app you send it with.
